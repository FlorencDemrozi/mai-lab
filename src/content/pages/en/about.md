## Why a dedicated lab

Research in biomedical engineering, AI, digital health, cognitive science and wearable
health at UiS has long been held back by scattered, project-specific equipment:
instruments bought for one study, hard to share between groups, and hard to combine
into the shared, multimodal datasets that modern health and AI research needs.

Much of our earlier data collection took place at Helse Campus Stavanger and in
hospitals. These remain valuable partners, but they are shared, heavily used clinical
spaces. Booking controlled sessions there is difficult, and outside activity can
compromise the experiment.

> A dedicated, controlled sensing environment at IDE/TN is a scientific and educational
> necessity, not a luxury.

The lab is anchored in IDE's Strategy 2026–2030, in two priority areas:
**Health & Welfare Technology** and **Fundamental & Applied AI**. The Head of
Department secured the physical space, and the Head of Engineers committed technical
support for installation, operation and maintenance.

## Teaching and student research

The lab gives students hands-on access to research-grade sensing equipment:

- Medical Technology BSc and AI/Kybernetikk MSc programmes
- BSc and MSc theses in health technology, signal processing, AI and embedded systems
- Ongoing PhD research
- The AI in Healthcare course (MTE105)

## From secured space to flagship lab

1. **Space secured.** Room KE E-470 confirmed, with technical support committed by the Head of Engineers.
2. **Day-one inventory.** Equipment contributed by team members forms the lab's initial instrumentation.
3. **Installation.** Instruments are installed and integrated into one synchronized sensing environment.
4. **Opening (target: end of 2026).** A shared, glass-fronted smart lab, visible to students and visitors.
5. **Growth.** Funded projects (NFR, EU Horizon, industry) add equipment over time.

## Open to the whole university

The lab is open to the wider Faculty of Science and Technology — Safety, Economics &
Planning; Mechanical & Structural Engineering; Energy & Petroleum Engineering;
Chemistry, Bioscience & Environmental Engineering; Mathematics & Physics — and beyond
it, to the Faculty of Health Sciences and the Faculty of Social Sciences.

It complements, rather than duplicates, UiS's Cognitive Lab and Public Health & Sports
Lab, and welcomes other Norwegian universities and international research partners.
