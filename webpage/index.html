Why a dedicated lab

Built out of a real constraint, not a wish list

Research spanning biomedical engineering, AI, digital health, cognitive science and wearable health at UiS has long been constrained by fragmented, project-specific equipment — instruments bought for one study, hard to share across groups, and hard to combine into the kind of shared, multimodal datasets modern health and AI research depends on.

Much of our prior data collection has depended on Helse Campus Stavanger and hospital environments. These remain valuable partners — but they are shared, heavily used clinical spaces, where scheduling controlled sessions is difficult and outside activity can introduce contamination risk to experimental validity.

A dedicated, controlled sensing environment at IDE/TN is a scientific and educational necessity — not a luxury.

What the lab holds

Instrumentation, by category

A deliberate mix of state-of-the-art research-grade systems — for publishable, clinically valid data — and accessible, lower-cost devices used for student training and for testing whether methods generalise to consumer-grade hardware.

Motion & biomechanics

  • Optical motion capture (Qualisys)
  • Markerless depth cameras
  • Full-body IMU (Xsens Awinda)
  • Force platforms & instrumented insoles

Physiological monitoring

  • Research-grade mobile EEG
  • Student open-source EEG unit
  • Wireless EMG & multimodal DAQ
  • ECG wearables & smartwatches

Cognitive & perceptual

  • Screen-based & wearable eye tracking
  • Wearable microphones
  • Room microphone array for speech/audio analysis

Sensing & localisation

  • UWB real-time location system
  • Thermal & optical cameras for contactless physiological sensing
  • 5G/6G sensing infrastructure (ISAC testbed)

Medical imaging

  • Portable point-of-care ultrasound
  • Portable low-field MRI for structural/functional neuroimaging outside a traditional MRI suite

Computing & lab environment

  • GPU workstation, NAS, edge nodes & networking
  • Perpetual licenses: eye tracking, EEG, motion capture
  • Fit-out: mounting rails, UPS, displays, acoustic treatment, furniture

Teaching & student research

A real hardware platform, not a demo cart

The lab supports the Medical Technology BSc and AI/Kybernetikk MSc programmes, giving students hands-on access to research-grade sensing equipment for coursework and thesis work in health tech, signal processing, AI and embedded systems.

  • Medical Technology BSc & AI/Kybernetikk MSc programmes
  • BSc & MSc thesis projects in health tech, signal processing, AI, embedded systems
  • Ongoing PhD research
  • AI in Healthcare course (MTE105)

Long-term vision

From secured space to flagship lab

  1. Step 1

    Space secured

    Room KE E-470 confirmed, with technical support committed by the Head of Engineers.

  2. Step 2

    Day-one inventory

    Existing equipment contributed by team members forms the lab’s initial instrumentation base.

  3. Step 3

    Installation

    Instrumentation installed and integrated into a single, coherent sensing environment.

  4. Step 4 · Target: end of 2026

    Opening

    The lab opens as a shared, glass-fronted smart lab, visible to students and visitors — IDE’s flagship environment for health and AI research.

  5. Step 5

    Growth

    Over time, funded projects — NFR, EU Horizon, industry — contribute further equipment through future roadmap cycles.

Collaboration & openness

Built to be shared

The lab is explicitly open to the wider TN faculty:

  • Safety, Economics & Planning
  • Mechanical & Structural Engineering
  • Energy & Petroleum Engineering
  • Chemistry, Bioscience & Environmental Engineering
  • Mathematics & Physics

And beyond TN, to the Faculty of Health Sciences and the Faculty of Social Sciences.

It’s designed to complement — not duplicate — UiS’s existing Cognitive Lab and Public Health & Sports Lab, and to welcome other Norwegian universities and international research partners.

Let’s talk before we open

We’re inviting academic researchers and industry partners to reach out about collaborating — joint R&D, shared studies, or clinical pilots — ahead of the lab’s opening.
